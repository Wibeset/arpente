Three great hikes in Québec

Last updated: November 21, 2026

From Gaspésie to the Saguenay Fjord, three classic Québec national park hikes worth a day of your life, and a page in your journal.

The trail pages

  • Mont Albert11.6 km return by the north side, or a 17.3 km loop
  • Acropole des DraveursAbout 10.4 km return to the first summit, 11.2 km to the third
  • Sentier de la StatueAbout 7.6 km return, or 3.6 km return to the Bellevue lookout

Mont Albert, Parc national de la Gaspésie

A long loop up to a vast bare plateau, where caribou sometimes graze above the treeline. Count about 17 km and 870 m of elevation gain, and 6 to 8 hours. The north side is open only part of the year to protect the caribou: check before you go.

Acropole des Draveurs, Parc national des Hautes-Gorges-de-la-Rivière-Malbaie

A steep climb, with stone steps, to a summit that looks straight down one of the deepest valleys east of the Rockies. Count about 10.4 km return to the first summit, around 800 m of elevation gain, and 4 to 6 hours. The trail closes for part of the year.

Sentier de la Statue, Parc national du Fjord-du-Saguenay

From Baie-Éternité, a climb through the forest to the statue of Notre-Dame-du-Saguenay, high above the fjord. Count about 7.6 km return and 3 to 4 hours, for roughly 600 m of elevation gain. A shorter option stops at the Bellevue lookout.

Distances, elevation gain and times are approximate, from park sources. Opening dates, access fees and trail conditions change: check sepaq.com before you go.

Keep the day

Tap record at the trailhead and put your iPhone in your pocket. Arpente draws your route and measures the distance, time and elevation gain, even with the screen off. Add a photo from the summit and a note on the way down, and the hike stays in your journal, next to the walks around your neighbourhood.

GPS can be less precise under thick trees or in deep valleys. For battery tips and more, see Support.
